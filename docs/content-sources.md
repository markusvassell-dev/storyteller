# Content sources & provenance

Every bundled asset must have documented source and rights metadata. This file
is the human-readable register; the machine-readable record is each book's
`rights` block in its `story.json`, enforced by `npm run audit:rights`.

## Original demonstration stories (6 books)

All six demo stories follow the same pattern:

- **Text**: an original retelling written specifically for this project. The
  underlying tale is traditional and centuries old (public domain as a tale);
  no published edition, translation, or modern adaptation was copied.
- **Artwork**: original flat-illustration SVG generated by this project's own
  art script (`scripts/generate-demo-art.ts`). Clearly labelled as
  project-created placeholder art; no external images used.
- **Rights status**: `original` — created for this project; may be replaced
  or modified freely; bundling and remote hosting allowed.

## Bundled public-domain classics (36 books)

Fetched by `npm run books:fetch` from Project Gutenberg (via its GitHub
mirror — see docs/research.md), normalised into the app's story format, and
validated by the build-blocking rights audit. Every edition below was
published before 1930 and is in the public domain in the United States; the
authors and illustrators died more than 70 years ago, so these are public
domain in life+70 jurisdictions as well.

Project Gutenberg's own header, footer and licence boilerplate are stripped
during import, so nothing carries their trademark or licence wrapper; the
canonical ebook page is recorded as each book's source. ⚠️ marks a book that
carries a content advisory (see below).

### Books carrying a content advisory

Two books contain period content worth knowing about. Their rights are
perfectly clear — this is a suitability question, recorded as
`contentAdvisory` rather than as a rights problem. **The owner reviewed both
and chose to show them**, so they sit in the library with a "note for
grown-ups" on their page (`advisoryAcknowledged: true`):

- **The Adventures of Tom Sawyer** (1876) — the unabridged text contains
  racial slurs and period racial attitudes.
- **Peter Pan** (1911) — depicts Native American characters with slurs and
  caricature. The gentler companion volume, *Peter Pan in Kensington
  Gardens*, is not hidden.

Either can be hidden again at any time from the owner workshop. Any *new*
book that acquires an advisory still defaults to hidden until someone
acknowledges it, so unreviewed period content cannot reach the library by
accident. Other
period content is noted without hiding: the Grimm collections keep their
original darkness, *The Jungle Book* and *Indian Fairy Tales* carry
colonial-era framing, *Treasure Island* has pirate violence, and *Black
Beauty* depicts animal cruelty deliberately.

## Candidate sources for future public-domain additions (not bundled)

When adding real public-domain editions later, verify the **specific edition
and translation**, record the source URL/organisation and the public-domain
basis, and note illustration provenance separately (illustrations often carry
different rights than the text):

- Project Gutenberg (https://www.gutenberg.org) — plain-text/HTML editions
  with per-book licence statements.
- Wikisource (https://en.wikisource.org) — transcriptions with edition info.
- Library of Congress digitised children's books — check each item's rights
  statement.

## Owner-imported books

Imported books (e.g. the owner's Robert Munsch collection) are **never**
bundled or uploaded. Their provenance is recorded per book in the import
wizard's Rights step (source of files, permission confirmation, personal-use
flag) and stays on-device. The rights audit will fail any attempt to move
`owner-permission` material into `public/stories/`.
